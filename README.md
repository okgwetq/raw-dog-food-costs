# raw dog food: what it costs per day, how to read a label, and how to earn cashback on every order

People searching for raw dog food usually aren't shopping for a philosophy. They've seen a friend's dog with a shinier coat, they've walked past a freezer of frozen chubs at the pet store, and they're stuck on two practical questions: does this actually do anything, and what will it do to my bank account?

The second question is the one that usually decides it. Raw feeding is the most expensive way to feed a dog in most markets, and it's almost always bought online, in bulk, on a subscription — which makes it one of the few pet categories where the purchasing mechanics matter as much as the food itself.

So here's the whole picture: what "raw" covers, what it costs in real numbers, how to spot a label that's genuinely complete, what the actual risks are, and how to claw back some of the spend on every order.

## Start with the format, because "raw" is four different products

Raw dog food gets treated as one category. It isn't. What you're buying changes the price, the freezer space, and how much of it you handle with bare hands.

**Frozen raw** is the original format. Ground meat, bone and organ, sold in chubs, patties or tubs, kept frozen until serving. Needs a freezer with room, and it's the messiest of the four.

**Freeze-dried raw** is raw food with the moisture pulled out. Shelf-stable, lightweight, and you rehydrate it with warm water before serving. Primal's Scoopable Pronto, for example, is a 16 oz bag you scoop, hydrate and serve; the beef recipe runs 39% minimum crude protein and 21% minimum fat, the chicken version 39% and 30%. The brand tells owners to use stainless steel, glass or silicone bowls rather than plastic or porcelain, and to refrigerate anything rehydrated and use it within five days.

**Dehydrated raw** sits between the two — dried at low heat rather than freeze-dried. Still shelf-stable, slightly different texture.

**DIY raw** is you at the butcher counter with a kitchen scale. It's the cheapest per pound and the only format where nothing is guaranteed, because the nutritional balance is entirely your problem.

The format matters for a second reason: three of those four are bought online, which is where cashback actually works. The exception is a local butcher or raw pet shop, and that's also the only one where a click-through can't help you.

## Two minutes on a label saves you from a bad bag

The single most useful thing on a raw package is the smallest block of text on it.

Look for a nutritional adequacy statement referencing AAFCO — the Association of American Feed Control Officials. It should say the food is complete and balanced for a specific life stage: growth, adult maintenance, or all life stages. Instinct's frozen FreshRaw Meals, for instance, are formulated to adult maintenance, and independent review site Dog Food Advisor rates the four recipes in that line between 4.5 and 5 stars, average 5, while flagging price as the main drawback.

If there's no such statement, you're feeding a supplement or a topper, not a diet. That's fine as long as you know it.

Then check the composition. A lot of freeze-dried raw advertises "85% meat and organs" with the remainder as produce, vitamins and minerals. It sounds like a purity number, but it's really telling you where the rest of the calories come from.

One more check: whether the manufacturer uses high-pressure processing, or HPP. It's a cold-water, high-pressure method — typically 80,000 to 87,000 psi for three to seven minutes, according to veterinary literature — that deactivates pathogens without cooking the food. Not every brand uses it, and the ones that do tend to print it on the bag.

Finally, read past the "no synthetic vitamins" line if you see one. Raw feeding has a real nutrient risk on the other side of that boast: taurine deficiency and mineral imbalance show up in raw diets that weren't formulated by someone who knew what they were doing.

## The part the raw food aisle doesn't put on the poster

Raw pet food is a global market worth roughly $3.1 billion, and it has been contested since the 1990s. The disagreement isn't about whether raw meat contains pathogens. It does. Salmonella, E. coli, listeria and campylobacter are routinely found in raw meat, and the FDA, CDC, American Veterinary Medical Association and World Small Animal Veterinary Association have all issued advisories against raw diets.

In early 2025 the argument got sharper. A raw turkey frozen food was recalled after testing positive for H5N1, and a house cat that ate it died; a second cat in California tested positive after eating a different raw brand, and two more cats in New York died in March 2025 in cases linked to yet another raw product. All of those cats were confirmed dead, and genetic testing matched one cat's virus to the food. Cats are particularly susceptible. Dogs can be infected but are less likely to become seriously ill.

Veterinarians generally point to fresh, gently cooked food as the lower-risk option that keeps most of what people like about raw: no preservatives, high digestibility, ingredient control. What raw has going for it — better coat, cleaner teeth, easier digestion — is mostly owner-reported rather than clinically demonstrated. A 2017 survey of 2,337 owners found that the most common source people used to learn about raw feeding was the internet, and that owners feeding raw diets trusted their vet's general advice less than owners feeding conventional diets did.

If you're going to feed raw anyway, the risk management is boring and it works: wash your hands and every surface the food touched, use stainless or glass bowls, keep the food frozen until serving, and don't let the dog lick your face mid-meal. If you have young children, immunocompromised people, or a cat in the house, that calculation deserves a serious second look before you buy anything.

## The small mistakes that kill pet food cashback

Raw orders are often large, and large orders are where tracking failures hurt most. None of these are exotic:

- **Starting at the retailer instead of the platform.** If you land on Chewy from a bookmark and remember about cashback afterwards, the attribution window is already gone.
- **Switching devices mid-purchase.** Start on the app, finish on the app. Start on desktop, finish on desktop.
- **Ad blockers, VPNs and privacy extensions.** These break the tracking cookie. Allowlist the cashback platform and the retailer, or turn them off for the order.
- **Non-approved promo codes.** Covered above, but it's the single most common cause of rejected pet food cashback.
- **Opens tabs and price comparisons mid-checkout.** Searching the product on Google or checking a competitor between activation and payment can detach the session.
- **Leaving the cart loaded before clicking through.** Chewy, PetSmart and Just Food For Dogs all list this in their terms — clear the cart, close the app, then click through and refill it.
- **Your store's country mismatching your account.** Buying from a foreign storefront usually means the cashback is rejected.
- **Returns and refunds.** If the frozen order arrives thawed and you send it back, that cashback goes with it. So does anything cancelled.

Cashback is also calculated on the order value, not on shipping or tax, and most retailers exclude delivery fees from the calculation. On a frozen raw subscription with shipping baked in, expect the payout to be a bit smaller than the headline percentage implies.

## Quick answers

**Is raw dog food worth the money?** That depends on your dog and your budget, and the honest answer is that the clinical evidence for the benefits is thinner than the marketing suggests, while the risks are real and documented. What isn't debatable is the cost: typically two to four times premium kibble per day, and more as the dog gets larger.
